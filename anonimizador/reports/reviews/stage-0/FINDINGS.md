## Grok round 9 (`grok-round9.md`, reviewed `6a9824c`)

Grok confirmed G0-29..G0-31 and reported five new findings, all valid. Three of them (G0-32, G0-35, G0-36) showed that each rule that tries to infer word boundaries in a content stream (a baseline, a line move, state across gaps) opens another layout that hides a short name. The content-stream reading was therefore redesigned to be conservative by construction, with no layout inference:

- Inside one text, strings that simply continue (adjacent `Tj`, small TJ kerning, text-state and color operators) are one word; another drawing (`ET`, `BT`, any other operator, binary data, a gap over 256 bytes) is a break.
- **Every move of the text position** (`Td`, `TD`, `Tm`, `T*`, `'`, `"`, a TJ adjustment of -200 or less) **is ambiguous**: only the fonts' widths, which are not read, tell glyph positioning, a word space, and a new line apart.
- Ambiguous moves are read joined, broken, and as every join of consecutive pieces up to 20 characters (`mixed_readings`). A short term or a number drawn in pieces is whole in one reading, whatever each move is. An ambiguous boundary can add a match, never hide one.
- The joins are searched only for terms that must be whole words (under 6 letters, `Term.whole_word`) and for patterns, so longer terms keep one count per occurrence.

This supersedes two earlier refutations: a string shown with `"` (G0-24) and a move to another line (G0-22) are now ambiguous too, and are counted. Counting them is the safe error. The refutation for strings drawn with no move at all (G0-22: `(Zu) Tj (leo) Tj (Assunto) Tj` is one word) still stands.
